# Standing decisions: a pending choice is state, continued by its own command

Resolved 2026-09-10, in-session triage of [#138](https://github.com/oscabriel/supervillain/issues/138), surfaced by the tobi/neohack source read (`~/Developer/clones/github.com/tobi/neohack` @ `b2e83ef`, clean, read-only). Companion decision: [ADR-0036](./0036-offers-eligibility-projection-on-the-model-entry.md). This amends nothing in the definition — it is the game-module seam gaining a written convention and a conformance exercise, with zero core code changes.

**Decision: a `ds.*` command needing a follow-up choice is a standing decision in `S`, continued by its own command.** The alternative shape — overloading the initiating command with one parameter per possible follow-up (`ds.attack {target, spendToken: true, …}`) — makes the Panel know every follow-up ahead of time and leaves the half-finished command in the Panel's local buffer, where a reconnect loses it. Under the standing-decision shape, the initiating command is accepted, `apply` leaves a decision in `S` with an id, a kind, and the offered answers, and a separate command (`ds.decision.answer {decisionId, answer}`, `ds.decision.cancel {decisionId}`) continues exactly that decision. neohack's rule is adopted as our own: an adapter may never substitute a newer standing question for a caller's older answer, and `decide` enforces that with one equality check on the id.

**Decision: at most one standing decision per seat, ids unique in the room's log.** neohack is one per game because one hero acts at a time; Draw Steel has simultaneous triggered actions across seats, so per-room is too narrow. The id is minted in `decide` — all randomness lives there (seam invariant 3) — and lands in the opening event's payload, so replay never re-mints it. Anything else the decision needs (kind, offered answers, the initiating command's context) is module-owned state inside `S`, opaque to the core per the standing seam discipline.

**Decision: the GM may resolve any seat's decision; a seat resolves its own.** This is seam invariant 4 (GM-in-the-loop) applied to stuck decisions rather than a new authority: every game this product serves has a GM, so a vanished player's pending choice is unstickable by definition. `decide` reads `TableView` (ADR-0002) to distinguish the seat's actor from the GM's.

**Decision: cancel cannot undo time already spent, and nothing auto-resolves.** neohack's line is adopted verbatim in spirit: cancelling a standing decision declines the pending choice; it does not roll back what the initiating command already applied. For Draw Steel that is the difference between "un-declare the attack" and "the attack happened, you declined the bonus." What a cancelled decision leaves behind is the module's per-decision-kind call. There is no timeout: no decision auto-resolves, ever — a stuck decision is resolved by a GM answer or cancel, never by the clock.

**Decision: the core stays blind (option 1 of the issue's three).** The standing decision lives in the module's `S`, typed only by the module; no core-owned decision envelope (option 3 — it drags a game concept into `core.*` and breaks namespace discipline), and no `GameView.standingDecision?()` hint yet (option 2 — recorded as named churn until a real Panel needs to show "waiting on <seat>" without importing `S`; strings-only if it lands, per ADR-0025/0031).

**Consequences:** the seam doc gains a Standing decisions section with these rules; `.agents/CONTEXT.md` pins **Standing decision** (avoid: pending command, unfinished action, modal, prompt state); the fake game module exercises the convention end to end — a two-step command (`fake.pledge`), the standing decision in `S`, a stale-id rejection, per-seat scope ("a seat with a standing decision cannot open a second one"), GM-vs-seat resolution rights, and a host-restart replay proving the decision survives in the log. The replay pillar (seam invariant 3: computed results live in Envelope payloads, replay never re-runs `decide`) is what makes all of this cheap — the decision is ordinary state, so it needs no engine pins, frozen calendars, or receipt chains of the kind neohack's re-execution architecture requires.

**Revisit triggers:** a Panel needs to show "waiting on \<seat\>" in the Feed or Sidebar without importing the module's `S` (→ adopt the option-2 `GameView` hint, read-only, strings only); a game demands more than one open decision per seat (→ widen the per-seat rule in the seam doc, not the shape); a real game needs decisions to expire (→ that is a GM-in-the-loop question first, a timeout mechanism second — the module may cancel on a GM command, the clock never resolves anything).
